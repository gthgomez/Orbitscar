# Competitive Landscape

**Status:** STRATEGIC RESEARCH — current snapshot
**Access date:** 2026-08-30

## Market shape

Sensor Tower reports that 2024 mobile-game IAP revenue returned to growth, with strategy among the growth-driving categories; its 2024 outlook identifies 4X strategy as the largest strategy subgenre and cites Whiteout Survival as a standout. This means money exists in the category, not that a small team can win distribution or content depth.

## Competitor patterns

### Clash of Clans

The official Builder Base documentation shows a strong attack/defense loop: brief preparation, selectable troops, staged attacks, defensive layouts, leagues, and friendly challenges. Its clarity and scale are formidable. We should learn from readable attacks and social testing; reject fantasy familiarity, enormous content breadth, and trophy-first motivation.

### Last War: Survival

Current season documentation shows large seasonal maps, faction alignment, connected territory, defense support, and alliance/faction technologies. Its scale and content cadence are difficult for an indie team to match. We should learn that seasons can refresh geography; reject hero/event bloat and power escalation.

### Whiteout Survival

Territory documentation emphasizes alliance territory, special structures, resource bonuses, passes/chokepoints, and state-vs-state conflict. It validates demand for geographic social play but also illustrates how alliance power and map administration can dominate the product.

### Star Trek Fleet Command

The official site combines base/fleet growth, exploration, recognizable IP, alliances, and PC/mobile availability. Its Territory Capture rules use adjacent zones, scheduled weekly takeovers, zone slots, alliance resources, and services. This is direct evidence that contiguous alliance territory is no longer a novel market claim by itself.

### Infinite Lagrange

Its official site sells a broad space-4X fantasy: fleet composition, alliance conquest, customization, exploration, faction interaction, mining, research, and base upgrading. We should narrow dramatically and make the colony attack itself the product.

### Rise of Kingdoms

The category is known for civilization progression, real-time marches, alliance geography, and a large social/meta layer. We should learn from asynchronous coordination and map stakes; reject empire-scale content and time commitment as the default.

### State of Survival

Community evidence describes alliance territories, state rules, and cross-state war, with players also describing heavy time and power expectations. Treat this as a warning that “social conflict” can become gatekeeping.

### Kingshot

Official positioning is a mobile strategy/simulation game; community tools show territory planning with HQs, flags, towns, and traps. The lesson is that players will externalize planning tools when in-game geography becomes complex. Our map must expose planning natively.

## Strategic conclusion

The category is crowded but legible. A defensible niche cannot be “sci-fi base building,” “alliances,” or “territory.” The narrower bet is:

> A readable, replayable, asynchronous fortification attack game where a 90-second breach and its report teach the player something, with a small alliance map that rewards geographic coordination without requiring a full-time officer class.

That niche remains unproven until strangers enjoy the same attack twice.
